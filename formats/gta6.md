# GTA 6 clip format guide

Use this guide for every batch. These are starting editorial settings. If you change a setting, give the revised guide to the full workflow for that batch.

## What makes a moment worth clipping

Scout the whole recording for anything worth replaying. Give priority to high-energy moments wherever they happen. Watch the game and facecam independently throughout: either can carry a clip on its own, and sometimes the timing between them makes the moment. Flag what catches your attention even during downtime. For each candidate, identify the frame or line that makes it worth watching and the setup a new viewer needs. Keep scouting after the first strong highlight.

Preserve the same visual format across clips and tailor the edit to each payoff. Give viewers a beat to spot a background detail, hold suspense through a near miss, or end a joke on the reaction. Keep every distinct clip that clears review, with no batch cap or category quota. When two clips score similarly, lead with a different kind of moment. A single chase can yield both a stunt and a later joke if each stands on its own.

## Picture

Create every edited preview and final clip on a 9:16 portrait canvas at 720p (720 pixels wide × 1280 pixels high). Apply this to gameplay-only, gameplay with facecam, and facecam-only edits. Verify the exported dimensions and square pixels before delivery. Export an MP4 with H.264 video, AAC audio and the source frame rate up to 60 fps. Stack the hook title at the top, then the facecam panel, then the captions, then the gameplay. The gameplay fills the bottom of the canvas edge to edge, cropped in from the sides just enough to fit the 9:16 frame, never showing the stream's own overlays or HUD widgets. Behind the hook, facecam and captions, fill the canvas with a synchronized duplicate, enlarged, blurred and darkened. Keep the action inside the crop; when two cars approach from opposite edges, show the entire wide shot instead. Zoom further only when the viewer can still see what causes the payoff.

Use Higgsfield Seedance 2.5 for all generated video elements. Select the exact model and request 9:16 at 720p. If the model is unavailable, mark generation pending. Preserve original stream footage and audio for gameplay and facecam edits.

## Facecam and gameplay

When a gameplay moment includes facecam, show both in the vertical edit. Start with the camera above the gameplay. Keep an embedded overlay in place if it is already readable and leaves the action visible. Play the original audio once and keep the face, speech and game action in sync.

Scout the camera throughout, including menus and quiet gameplay. Flag funny lines, interesting observations, expressions and unexpected activity in the room. If the moment makes sense with the gameplay hidden, make a separate facecam-only candidate. Use the clean camera track when available, or crop the original overlay at a readable size. Keep enough of the room in frame to see what happened and keep subtitles clear of it. Use the camera image for any background fill.

Rank camera-only clips alongside the gameplay edits. Two clips can share a timestamp when each delivers a distinct payoff, such as an in-game stunt and a pet knocking something over behind the streamer. If a reaction needs the game to explain it, keep the combined edit.

## Timing and hook

Let each joke set its own runtime. Open on the first line or action that makes a new viewer curious. Keep the setup needed to understand the turn, including a pause, glance or failed attempt when it builds anticipation. Give the punchline and the streamer's genuine reaction room to land. If the streamer adds a second laugh with a comeback, gesture or excuse, stay for it. End as soon as the last funny beat registers. A quick gag can run under 30 seconds and a sustained escalation can run over 45. Judge both by the payoff and pacing.

Keep the facecam prominent when the expression carries the joke. Chat can flag the moment. Time subtitles to the actual delivery so the punchline first appears when it is spoken. Keep any opening text short and spoiler-free.

## Captions and sound

Use Barlow Bold in sentence case. On a 720 × 1280 canvas, start at 50 px for subtitles and 58 px for hooks, with white lettering, a 3 px charcoal outline and a subtle shadow. Use warm yellow on one word when emphasis helps a line land. Set two lines maximum, break at natural phrases, and preview at phone size. Keep captions steady through delivery. Keep text inside the middle 80% of the frame width and above the bottom 20%. Move it when it covers a face, map marker or the action. Transcribe the actual speech, verify names by ear and time each phrase to its speaker. Preserve a silent visual gag. Prioritize dialogue and meaningful game sounds. Add a quiet licensed accent only when it draws attention to something real. Omit added music when the original reaction carries the scene.

## What keeps a viewer watching

The Scout identifies the reason to stay after the first frame and the exact moment the viewer gets the payoff. The Editor opens on that question or action, cuts repeated information, and keeps any pause that builds anticipation. The Producer watches once as a newcomer and marks the first point where interest slips. Test a shorter setup or clearer opening when needed, then check that the reaction still lands. Use actual audience retention when available to evaluate these judgments.

## Selection and delivery

Keep distinct moments a new viewer can understand. One event earns one posting slot unless a second cut tells a genuinely different story. Deliver a playable MP4 per winner, a short title, source credit, a source timestamp and one sentence explaining the choice. Hold a clip with unresolved publishing permission or unclear third-party audio for review. Keep drafts separate from the posting queue.

## Example edit

Illustrative footage: a driver says “I know a shortcut,” turns down an alley, and meets the same police cruiser at the other end. Start on “shortcut.” Keep both the alley entrance and the cruiser visible. Caption the line, let the sound of the brakes carry the reveal, and end after the driver’s reaction. A fitting title is “He knew a shortcut.” A spike of laughter in chat is a cue to rewind and find the turn.

Paste: “Run my GTA 6 clipping workflow on [link]. Use the clip format guide. Make every distinct clip that clears review and return the ranked MP4s.” Attach an updated copy of this guide to override the bundled settings. An accessible recording works for a first run. For a broadcast in progress, live capture and timestamped chat must be available to the session or supplied by a recorder. If available, include a source credit and a timestamped chat export with the recording.
